# /the-game/overview

This page gives you the complete game on a single screen. Every system mentioned here has its own dedicated page below if you want to dive deeper.

## The core loop

At its heart, Dealers.sh is a price arbitrage game wrapped in a mafia fiction. You move between areas, buy drugs where they are cheap, sell them where they are expensive, and reinvest the profits into bigger and bigger plays. The complication is that every action generates police heat, every area has different opportunities and risks, and other players in your area can attack you once you start to look like a worthwhile target.

A typical session moves through three stages. You begin on the streets with PVE (player versus environment) deals that build your reputation and your cash position. Once your reputation crosses a threshold, PVP (player versus player) opens up and you can start stealing from other dealers in your area. Heists sit at the top of the curve and pay the largest amounts, but they require the most setup and carry the most downside.

The shorthand we use for this is "PVE for the rep, PVP for the infamy, heists for the bag," and the rest of this section explains what each of those words actually means.

## The five numbers that matter

Almost everything in Dealers.sh moves one or more of the following stats. Knowing what each one does and what changes it is the fastest way to feel in control of the game.

| Stat | What it represents | What changes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reputation (REP)** | Your standing in the family. Drives ranks and gates PVP. | Earned through PVE wins, PVP wins, achievements. Lost on PVE failures and PVP losses. |
| **Heat** | Police attention, scaled 0 to 5. At 5 you go to jail. | Most deals add a little. Failed deals add a lot. The Clear Heat action lowers it. |
| **Cash** | Liquid working capital. | Spent on drugs, travel, boosts, and bail. Earned from sells, PVP loot, and heists. |
| **Stash** | Drugs you currently hold, by type and quantity. | Goes up when you buy or win a PVP fight. Goes down when you sell or lose one. |
| **Attempts** | Actions remaining in the current period. | Each buy or sell costs one. Refills on a daily timer or via a shop reset. |

There is a sixth stat, Infamy, that is specific to PVP. It tracks how much other dealers fear or respect you, and it moves up or down based on the outcomes of fights. See [/the-game/pvp](/the-game/pvp) for the details.

## The tabs

The game UI is divided into a small number of tabs, each documented in its own page.

The dashboard tab, [Stash](/the-game/stash), is your default view and surfaces all of the stats above in one place. From there, four tabs handle actions: [Deal](/the-game/deal) covers PVE buying and selling, [Move](/the-game/move) handles travel and area changes, [PVP](/the-game/pvp) lets you attack other dealers, and [Heist](/the-game/heist) is reserved for larger coordinated jobs.

Two more tabs handle utilities. The [Shop](/the-game/shop) sells time-limited boosts and attempt resets, and the [Pager](/the-game/pager) is where notifications and claimable achievements appear. Finally, [Jail](/the-game/jail) is a state rather than a tab in the strict sense, but it has its own page because the rules change while you are inside.

## How the systems interact

The systems are designed to pull against each other. Successful PVE adds reputation but almost always adds some heat as well, so a long PVE session without ever pausing to clear heat will eventually put you in jail. PVP wins pay reputation and infamy at the same time, but losing PVP fights costs both, and the more infamous you are the more often other players will target you. The Move tab is the lever you use to manage all of these tradeoffs: a Safe House cools your heat without costing cash, a Black Market gives you different selling prices, and a different street gives you fresh PVP targets to scout.

The shop boosts (Grinder, Hustler, and Kingpin) multiply gains for a fixed window of time, which is why they are most valuable when you are running a known-profitable strategy, not when you are gambling on a single risky deal.
